The column is **kept** rather than discarded. Although coverage is poor
overall, it is complete for the Premier League, which preserves a
full referee-level analysis for that league. Every referee
visualisation is explicitly scoped to the Premier League and states
this limitation on screen, so the restricted coverage cannot be
mistaken for a league-wide result.

### 3b. Fouls — a systematic gap, not random missingness

Fouls are missing for **385** matches. These are
not scattered at random; they are concentrated in whole seasons where
the source never recorded the statistic:

| League | Season | Matches with no foul data |
|---|---|---:|
| French Ligue 1 | 2006/07 | 380 |

These values are **left missing and never imputed**. Filling them
(with zero or with a mean) would fabricate a foul rate for a season
that has none, and would distort any home-versus-away foul comparison
that included it. Charts involving fouls exclude these seasons.

## 5. Crowd status classification

The project's central question is whether home advantage depends on the
crowd. The source data has no attendance column, so each match is
classified by the conditions it was played under:

| Label | Window | Matches |
|---|---|---:|
| Crowds present (pre-COVID) | before 08 Mar 2020 | 25,034 |
| Empty / restricted stadiums | 08 Mar 2020 – 30 Jun 2021 | 2,255 |
| Crowds returned (post-COVID) | after 30 Jun 2021 | 8,908 |

**This is a documented approximation.** Leagues suspended play in March
2020; four resumed behind closed doors while Ligue 1 abandoned its
season. The 2020/21 season was played predominantly without spectators,
with limited and inconsistent partial attendance in some countries late
in the season. Because policy varied by country and by club, the
boundaries above cannot be exact, and the assumption is stated in the
application itself rather than hidden in the pipeline.

## 6. Transformations applied

1. **Merge** — 100 season files across 5 league folders, each validated
   against the expected 22-column schema before use.
2. **Season labelling** — filenames converted to `2006/07` style labels,
   with a numeric `season_start_year` so seasons sort chronologically
   rather than alphabetically.
3. **Column renaming** — source abbreviations mapped to clean snake_case
   names, with display labels held separately for the interface.
4. **Type correction** — count statistics stored as nullable integers so
   that absent values remain absent instead of being coerced to zero,
   which would bias every average computed from them.
5. **Derived measures** — points (3/1/0), goal difference, total goals,
   shot accuracy, shot conversion, and home-minus-away differentials for
   shots, corners, fouls and cards.
6. **Crowd-status flag** — each match labelled by attendance conditions,
   creating the natural experiment the project analyses.
7. **Pivot to long format** — the match-level table is reshaped to one
   row per team per match with a `venue` field. This turns every
   home-versus-away comparison into a single group-by and is the basis
   of the team-level dashboard.
8. **Output** — both tables written as Parquet, which preserves dtypes
   (CSV does not) and loads substantially faster in the application.
